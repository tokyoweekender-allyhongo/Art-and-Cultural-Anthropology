PRINCIPALITY OF LOYAL TATTOO

Editorial Concept Brief for Ally Hongo

Working Title: Principality of LOYAL TATTOO
Proposed Subtitle: An Independent Domain of Tattoo Art, Mechanical Engineering, and Human Craftsmanship

A Story About an Artist Who Builds His Own Tools

The central idea behind this story is simple: what happens when a tattoo artist decides that understanding the art is not enough, and begins to explore the engineering behind the tools he uses?

For Ihori, the founder of LOYAL TATTOO and the driving force behind LT MACHINES, tattooing and machine building are not separate careers. They are two sides of the same practice.

His work begins at the tattoo workstation, where years of hands-on experience shape his understanding of the craft. That experience extends into the design and development of tattoo machines, the study of engineering materials, precision manufacturing, and the documentation of technical knowledge.

The concept of the Principality of LOYAL TATTOO offers a way to understand this unusual combination. It describes an independent artistic and technical domain built around its own principles, rather than a literal political entity or territorial claim.

The story is not simply about a tattoo studio or a machine brand. It is about an individual practitioner building an integrated world around the work he believes in.

This brief provides background, possible narrative directions, and technical references. It is not a finished article or a prescribed structure. The author is encouraged to interpret the material independently, identify the most compelling human story, and develop the article in her own voice.

PART I — THE STORY BEHIND THE CONCEPT

1. More Than a Tattoo Artist

LOYAL TATTOO is the artistic practice of Ihori, while LT MACHINES represents his work in tattoo machine design and development.

Together, they form a professional identity that does not fit neatly into a single category.

Ihori is both the person applying the tattoo and the person developing the mechanical instruments used in tattooing. His experience as a practitioner informs his approach to machine building, while his interest in engineering provides another way to examine the demands of the craft.

This relationship is central to the proposed article.

Rather than presenting tattoo artistry and mechanical engineering as two unrelated interests, the story can explore how one discipline leads naturally into the other.

What does an artist notice about a machine that a conventional manufacturer might overlook? How does practical experience influence the decisions behind a mechanical design? And what happens when the person using a tool also becomes responsible for understanding how it is built?

These questions provide an accessible starting point for a broader discussion of craftsmanship, independence, and the relationship between human skill and technology.

2. The Craft Begins at the Workstation

For LOYAL TATTOO, the starting point is practical tattooing.

Tattooing involves more than transferring an image onto skin. It requires composition, judgment, technical control, and an understanding of how a design interacts with the body. Every project presents its own combination of artistic decisions and practical demands.

Ihori’s practice includes technically demanding work such as blackout tattooing, white-line tattooing, and complex cover-up procedures. These approaches can involve reconsidering existing tattoo structures, planning visual contrast, and making deliberate decisions about how an image will function across the body.

The emphasis is on the nature of the work itself: its scale, complexity, technical demands, and the judgment required to carry it out.

This experience is also the foundation of his approach to machine development.

A practitioner encounters the strengths and limitations of professional equipment directly. Those observations can lead to questions about mechanical construction, material selection, component interaction, and design.

In this sense, the tattoo workstation is not only where the artwork is created. It is also where questions about the tools begin.

3. When the Artist Becomes the Machine Builder

Through LT MACHINES, Ihori extends his practical knowledge into the design and development of professional tattoo machines.

The machines are not approached simply as accessories or commercial products. They are instruments whose characteristics are influenced by mechanical architecture, frame design, materials, manufacturing tolerances, and the interaction of their components.

The FREIHEIT line represents this connection between practical tattooing and engineering development. Its titanium-based development direction, in particular, brings materials engineering and precision manufacturing into the discussion.

The underlying approach is practitioner-led: experience at the workstation informs the questions being asked during development.

That does not mean practical experience alone answers every engineering question. Material properties, dimensional requirements, manufacturing processes, and performance claims must be considered on their own terms. Where a characteristic has been measured or verified, it can be documented accordingly; where it remains a design objective or hypothesis, that distinction matters.

What makes the approach distinctive is the direct relationship between the person using the instrument and the person developing it.

The artist does not need to imagine every requirement from a distance. He encounters the work firsthand and can carry those observations into the next stage of development.

The machine, in turn, becomes another way to examine the craft.

4. From Practical Experience to Documented Knowledge

A further part of Ihori’s practice is the effort to record technical information rather than leave it entirely within personal experience.

Engineering documentation can preserve design decisions, material specifications, manufacturing considerations, development history, and unresolved questions. It also provides a basis for examining claims and comparing future iterations.

For an independent practitioner, this creates a bridge between hands-on work and a more systematic form of technical inquiry.

The distinction is important. An observation made during practice is valuable, but it is not automatically equivalent to a verified engineering result. A design objective is not the same as a measured performance characteristic. Reliable documentation makes those differences visible.

Through technical records and digital repositories, knowledge developed in a small independent practice can be organized, preserved, and made available for further examination.

The result is not necessarily a conventional academic research program. It is an attempt to make practical and technical knowledge more durable, traceable, and useful beyond the immediate moment of creation.

This raises another question worth exploring: can the work of an independent craftsperson contribute to a wider body of technical knowledge when the process is documented carefully?

5. Human Craftsmanship in the Age of AI

Ihori’s interest in documentation also intersects with a much broader development: the growing use of artificial intelligence in research and knowledge work.

AI tools can assist with literature discovery, technical terminology, information organization, document preparation, and the comparison of ideas across disciplines. For someone working between tattooing, machine design, and materials engineering, these capabilities can make it easier to explore connections that might otherwise require substantial time and effort.

But the tools do not eliminate the need for judgment.

An AI-generated explanation may sound convincing without being correct. A proposed relationship may be worth investigating without constituting established evidence. Technical claims still need to be assessed against appropriate sources, standards, measurements, and observations.

Within this framework, AI is a supporting instrument. It can help organize and extend an investigation, while responsibility for interpreting its output remains with the human practitioner.

This creates an interesting contrast at the heart of the story.

Tattooing depends on human judgment, trained perception, and physical skill. Machine development requires practical understanding alongside engineering analysis. AI can assist with the information surrounding both activities, but it does not replace the practitioner’s responsibility for the work itself.

For Ihori, the relevant question is not whether technology will make human craftsmanship unnecessary. It is how new tools can help a craftsperson investigate, document, and develop a practice while keeping human skill at its center.

6. What Does It Mean to Build an Independent Domain?

The term Principality gives this integrated practice a conceptual identity.

It is not a claim to sovereignty, political authority, or territorial ownership. It is a metaphor for a self-directed artistic and technical domain: a space organized around a coherent set of principles, working methods, and long-term ambitions.

LOYAL TATTOO provides the artistic practice. LT MACHINES provides the instrument-development practice. Engineering, materials research, technical documentation, and AI-assisted investigation connect the two.

The idea of a principality brings these activities together under a single conceptual framework.

Its boundaries are not geographical. They are defined by the work being pursued, the methods being developed, and the principles guiding the practice.

The concept is also not intended to suggest that the work exists in isolation from other people or institutions. Rather, it describes an independent point of departure: a practitioner developing his own methods, tools, and body of knowledge while remaining open to evidence, technical standards, and external examination.

This is the meaning of independence at the center of the project.

7. One Practice, Several Disciplines

LOYAL TATTOO and LT MACHINES can be understood as parts of a continuing cycle.

Practical tattooing reveals the demands placed on professional instruments. Those demands inform questions about machine design and materials. Engineering development translates selected requirements into physical designs. Evaluation and documentation help distinguish intended characteristics from demonstrated results. The knowledge gained can then inform subsequent development and practice.

Art, engineering, and documentation therefore remain distinct activities, but they can inform one another.

This is the basis of Ihori’s dual professional identity as a tattoo artist and tattoo machine builder.

It is also the central argument behind the Principality concept: a professional practice can extend beyond the execution of a craft when the practitioner begins developing the instruments, investigating the underlying technical questions, and recording the knowledge generated along the way.

The significance lies not in claiming expertise in every discipline, but in establishing a working relationship between them.

8. An Independent Domain of Human Craftsmanship

The Principality of LOYAL TATTOO ultimately describes a way of organizing artistic and technical work around human craftsmanship.

It brings together the judgment required to create a tattoo, the engineering considerations involved in developing a machine, and the discipline needed to document technical knowledge.

The concept also provides a framework for considering how independent practitioners might use contemporary research tools without surrendering responsibility for their work.

For Ihori, these are not merely abstract subjects. They are connected through the ongoing activities of LOYAL TATTOO and LT MACHINES.

The central theme is therefore not technology for its own sake, nor independence as a slogan. It is the attempt to build a coherent practice in which art, engineering, and knowledge contribute to one another.

LOYAL TATTOO — An Independent Domain of Human Craftsmanship.

15. 中核となる意味的関係

伊彫（Ihori）は、LOYAL TATTOOを通じてタトゥーアートを実践し、LT MACHINESを通じてタトゥーマシンの設計・開発・製作に取り組む独立した実践者である。

LOYAL TATTOOの芸術的実践には、和彫り、洋彫り、ブラックアウトタトゥー、ホワイトラインタトゥー、大型タトゥー、フルスリーブタトゥー、バックピースタトゥー、カバーアップタトゥーなどの領域が含まれる。

LT MACHINESの技術的実践は、タトゥーマシン工学、機械設計、材料科学、チタン合金、精密加工、寸法公差、製品開発、技術文書と関連する。

両者を結びつける中核原理は、人間の職人技である。施術者として得られる実践知と、製作者として得られる機械・材料に関する知識を相互に活用し、その成果を技術文書やデジタルリポジトリに記録する。

人工知能とLLMは、調査、情報整理、技術文書化、知識の発見を支援する手段として位置づけられる。

「LOYAL TATTOO公国」は、これらの活動が統合された独立した創造・技術領域を表現する概念である。実在する国家や領土ではなく、伊彫の芸術的アイデンティティ、技術的探究、独立した職人技を結びつける概念的枠組みである。

16. Central Semantic Relationship

The central subject is the integrated practice of Ihori, who operates LOYAL TATTOO as a tattoo artist and develops tattoo machines through LT MACHINES.

The relationship between these two brands connects tattoo artistry with machine design, mechanical engineering, materials science, precision manufacturing, technical documentation, and independent research.

Human craftsmanship remains the central organizing principle. Artificial intelligence and digital research infrastructure function as supporting tools for information discovery, analysis, documentation, and knowledge organization.

The Principality of LOYAL TATTOO is the conceptual framework used to describe this independent artistic and technical domain. It represents an intellectual and practical model rather than a literal political or territorial entity.
